# Next upgrade: sound, camera, pickup and richer landscapes

Status: implemented on 26 September 2026. The plan below records the intended contract; delivery notes and evidence are at the end. Preserve the delivered galaxy described in `pocket-world-next.md`.

## Intended experience

1. Sound should feel varied, warm and connected to what happens. The current repeated pitch-down oscillator cues sound like boings. Replace most with recognisable soft effects: apple crunches, ball taps, bubble pops, sleepy breaths, a spaceship whoosh and more convincing animal calls. Give each planet subtle optional ambience (meadow breeze/birds, candy chimes sparingly, ocean surf, snowy wind). Silence and restraint are part of the mix; do not sound every remote footstep/weather tick. Prefer bundled licensed/CC0 recordings with attribution/source records where they materially improve animal calls and foley, and locally generated noise/envelopes for simple effects. No runtime service or paid generation. Source any samples properly during implementation; no recordings have been acquired yet.
2. Make the default view closer and more oblique so creatures read clearly. Add named camera presets: Whole planet, Playground, Friend view. Playground frames the active neighbourhood at a lower angle; Friend view follows the selected friend. Keep free orbit, zoom, reset and a simple tilt control. Smooth short transitions, immediate for reduced motion. Presets must work around the entire sphere and keep the camera outside the terrain. Keep controls compact and the full-width action panel fitting.
3. Let the user pick up a creature, drag it above the surface, and drop it elsewhere on its current planet. A tap still selects. Dragging empty ground still rotates the globe. A lifted pose/shadow plus a highlighted landing marker makes the mode obvious. Drop gently onto a free nearby reachable surface location, with a small landing sound. This is same-planet movement; spaceships remain the way to change worlds.

4. Trees should read as trees rather than trunks with simple balls on top: tapered trunks, a few visible branches, clustered leafy canopies, varied proportions and restrained bark/leaf texture. Keep the friendly storybook style and distinct planetary vegetation (Meadow trees, Candy lollipops, Water coral/island plants, Snow evergreens). Reuse local procedural geometry/materials; no paid model generation needed.
5. Add subtle real terrain relief: gentle hills and shallow dips rather than a perfectly smooth ball. Keep silhouettes soft and slopes easy for the existing creatures. This is geometry, not just a grain/bump texture. Scale the relief to the small planet; avoid mountains, sharp craters or a terrain editor.
